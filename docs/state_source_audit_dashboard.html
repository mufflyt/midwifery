Section 2

Agreement with NPPES, where a source repeats

DAC is the longest-running source besides NPPES itself: a tight 91.8–95.1% band for thirteen straight years, then a single lower year in 2026 — not a trend, at least not yet. Open Payments stays high across its real years; PECOS revalidation stays flat and distinctly lower than everything else, every one of its six years; Facility Affiliation — newly extended back to 2013 from CCNs embedded in the historical DAC files — sits consistently highest of all four, 92.7–96.0%, with its own real gap, 2023–2025.

Figure 2. Agreement with NPPES, multi-year sources, with 95% confidence bands. Four lines plot the % of same-year comparable NPI-records whose state matches NPPES that year, for the sources with enough years to trend: DAC/Physician Compare (2013–2026, 14 points), Open Payments (2021–2025, 5 points — 2015–2020 excluded as too small to plot meaningfully, see Figure 1), PECOS revalidation (2021–2026, 6 points), and Facility Affiliation (2013–2022 solid, then a dashed jump to an isolated 2026 point — CMS dropped the embedded CCN field from the DAC file in 2023, see Figure 1). Y-axis runs 80–100%. Band width tracks sample size directly: Open Payments' ribbon (n≈14,000–22,000/year) is barely visible, while DAC's and Facility Affiliation's thinner years (n≈700–6,500) show a perceptibly thicker band. Open Payments contributes noticeably more comparable volume than DAC or PECOS in most years, which is part of why its agreement line (§2) looks more stable — the same few percentage points of disagreement move around less in a bigger denominator. DAC's volume itself halves from 2013 to the late 2010s as the cohort-matching base shrank, then partly recovers — without a matching swing in its agreement rate, so the two aren't mechanically linked here. PECOS revalidation's volume climbs steadily 2021→2025 then dips slightly in 2026, while its agreement rate barely moves at all — the flat line in §2 isn't a sign of a small or shrinking cohort.

Every number so far is a source compared against NPPES — never against each other, the gap this audit originally flagged. Does license status predict disagreement?

A direct test of a question this audit had only been able to flag as unresolved, every time it came up: is cross-source disagreement a sign of genuine multi-state practice, or a data-quality problem indistinguishable from one? CMS's Open Payments provider-profile registry — data/CMS_Open_Payments_Profile_Supplement.csv, already in the repo but previously used only for its name/address fields — carries which states each provider is licensed in, independent of every source already in this audit. Single-state-licensed midwives are 92.4% Unanimous and 2.4% Split. Multi-state-licensed midwives are 59.9% Unanimous and 22.1% Split — roughly nine times the single-state rate. The association is not subtle: χ²(2, n=8,312) = 1,353.1, p < 2.2×10⁻¹⁶. This doesn't prove any one individual disagreement is a real multi-state practice rather than a data error — it's a population-level association, not a per-NPI adjudication — but it strongly supports genuine multi-state practice as the dominant driver of cross-source disagreement, not an edge case this audit could previously only gesture at.

Seven stacked bars, one per year, each divided into six categories from R/lib/medicaid_state_concordance.R's classify_state_concordance(): Concordant and Multistate concordant (both green — the practice state matches NPPES, either in one state or across a documented multi-state enrollment), Discordant and Multistate discordant (both terracotta — does not match), Indeterminate (light grey, not resolvable), and Not observed (dark grey, no Medicaid enrollment record exists for that NPI-year at all). Section 7

What this does not establish

01

Pairwise still means pairwise, not against truth. §4 adds 36 non-NPPES pairs (every pair with ≥30 comparable NPI-years, via mysteryroster::attribute_agreement()), but a few pairs still have no comparison at all — PECOS revalidation and State Boards of Nursing share zero NPIs in this cohort — and no pair here, NPPES-anchored or not, tells you which side of a disagreement is correct.

02

State Boards of Nursing cover 7 states — Colorado, Connecticut, Florida, Illinois, Pennsylvania, Texas, Washington — not a 50-state audit for that source.

03

NPPES's own staleness isn't classified. last_update_date exists in the raw NPPES files but isn't yet extracted into midwife_panel.csv, so a disagreement here could be NPPES being stale, the other source being stale, or a genuine multi-state practice — this audit can't tell which.

04

Healthgrades and the BON data are single, non-reproducible scrapes. Re-running next year shows whether the scrape changed, not whether the underlying record did.

05

Open Payments' N counts payment transactions, not NPI-years. A provider with five payments in one year contributes up to five comparison rows in §2 and §4 alike — consistent with how this audit has always counted Open Payments, but a different unit than every other source's one-row-per-NPI-year.

06

§5's 5-year flow cohort is 184 NPIs out of a ~5,000–10,000/year panel — requiring all four longitudinal sources present every single year from 2021–2025 is a demanding filter, and that 184 is not necessarily representative of midwives who only appear in some years or some sources.

07

Medicaid is excluded from §4's consensus score too, same reason as the pairwise matrix above: its source here is a pre-built Concordant/Discordant classification, not a raw per-NPI-year state value.

08

Facility Affiliation's 2013–2022 state is an arbitrary tie-break, not a collapse rule. A midwife with CCNs resolving to hospitals in two different states in the same year gets whichever CCN appeared first in the source file, not a documented "primary" affiliation — CMS gives no rank or admission volume to break the tie honestly. n_ccns_resolved is kept in the underlying data so the multiplicity stays visible even though the audit's state comparison collapses it to one value.
