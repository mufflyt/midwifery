## Corrections applied while rebuilding this checklist

- **Item 10 (study size).** Added an explicit sentence to Methods → Cohort and follow-up (`manuscript/midwife_persistence.qmd`) stating that no sample-size calculation was performed and that the analysis is a complete enumeration. This fact previously appeared only in Discussion → Strengths and limitations ("an enumeration rather than a sample"), which is the wrong section for a STROBE reviewer to find it in — item 10 is a Methods item.
- **R Core Team citation year.** `manuscript/references.bib`'s `@rlang` entry was dated 2024. The manuscript cites R by `` `r K$meta$r_version` `` at render time, which resolves to the R version actually used to run the analysis — **R 4.6.1, a 2026 release** (confirmed via `R.version.string` and `citation()` on the rendering machine). Updated the entry to `year = {2026}` and added the DOI `10.32614/R.manuals` that `citation()` reports for that version. A citation dated two years earlier than the software version named beside it in the same sentence is exactly the kind of small, checkable inconsistency a STROBE-conscious reviewer looks for.

## Issues found, not fixed (require author input or a judgment call, not a mechanical correction)

1. **Item 22 (Funding) — unfilled placeholder.** `manuscript/title_page.qmd` states `**Funding:** [To be completed.]`. This cannot be resolved without the authors specifying an actual funding source (or "none").
2. **Adjacent submission fields also unfilled**, though not literally STROBE items: `**Financial Disclosure:** [To be completed. The authors did not report any potential conflicts of interest.]` and `**Presented at:** [None / to be completed.]` on the title page. Worth resolving alongside item 22 before submission, since a reviewer checking Funding will see both.
3. **Item 1(a) — design term not in the title.** The title is a plain descriptive title; "retrospective cohort study" appears only in the Abstract's Methods sentence. STROBE requires the term in the title *or* the abstract, so this technically satisfies the checklist as written — flagged only because some journals' own house style additionally prefers the design term in the title itself. A judgment call for the authors, not a defect.
4. **Item 12(d) — loss to follow-up (cohort-specific wording).** The manuscript states providers "were not required to appear in every year" and that "entry and exit are themselves observed quantities," but does not report a specific loss-to-follow-up rate (e.g., what fraction of the cohort was observed only in early snapshots and never reappears) the way the cohort-specific STROBE wording anticipates. This may be intentional, since the outcome here is persistence rather than a time-to-event endpoint where attrition has a more standard meaning — but it is worth a one-sentence author decision on whether to add a quantified loss-to-follow-up statement or note explicitly why the concept does not transfer cleanly to this design.
5. **Item 15 — outcome reported at two horizons, not "over time."** The cohort-specific wording asks for outcome measures "over time"; this manuscript reports annual and career persistence as two aggregate horizons rather than a year-by-year trend across the full 2007–2025 span. Given the study's own two-horizon framing is the paper's central methodological point (short-horizon vs. career-horizon persistence diverge), this is very likely intentional and defensible as written — flagged for the authors to confirm rather than treated as a defect.
6. **`@nppes` bibliography entry's date range (2007–2025)** should be spot-checked against the panel's actual `panel.year_min`/`panel.year_max` values at the next full render (these require the gitignored person-level panel and could not be independently verified in this pass without running the pipeline).

## Not applicable to this design

- **Item 6(b)** (matching criteria for matched cohorts) — not a matched design.
- **Item 16(c)** (translating relative risk to absolute risk) — the outcome is a retention proportion, not a relative risk.
